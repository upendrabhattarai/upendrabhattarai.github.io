Education

PhD in Genetics
2019 — 2023
University of Otago, New Zealand

Investigated host–parasite molecular interactions using genomics, transcriptomics, and epigenetics. Applied RNA-seq, Nanopore sequencing, bisulfite sequencing, and network analysis.

PhD in Agricultural Entomology and Pest Control
2014 — 2018
Northwest A&F University, China

Studied host manipulation in viral-infected lepidopterans. Used genome-wide transcriptomics and functional genomics to study circadian rhythms and phototransduction mechanisms.

MSc in Plant Science
2010 — 2012
Norwegian University of Life Sciences, Norway

Focused on interactions between fungal pathogens and their host mites in the context of behavioural manipulation.

BSc in Agriculture
2005 — 2009
Tribhuvan University, Nepal

Bachelor's degree in Agriculture.

Professional experience

Research Associate
Mar 2024 — Present
Harvard Chan Bioinformatics Core · Department of Biostatistics, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, Boston, MA

Provide bioinformatics consultation and technical support to researchers across Harvard. Develop and deliver workshops on bioinformatics tools, data analysis, and best practices. Collaborate with interdisciplinary teams to analyse and interpret high-throughput sequencing and omics data, and help optimise computational workflows for large-scale biological datasets — across human, mouse, and diverse organism models.

Postdoctoral Fellow
May 2022 — Mar 2024
Extavour Lab · Department of Organismic and Evolutionary Biology, Harvard University, Cambridge, MA

Developed five hemimetabolous insect species as genetic model organisms. Assembled chromosome-level genomes, annotated, and carried out comparative genomic and transcriptomic analyses to identify evolutionary patterns in gene regulation across temporal and spatial scales.

Research Technician
2012 — 2013
Norwegian Institute for Agricultural and Environmental Research (Bioforsk), Ås, Norway

Performed laboratory experiments across three trophic levels (fungus, mites, plants). Maintained cultures of acaropathogenic fungi, mites, flies, and plants, and conducted experiments on host–pathogen interactions.
