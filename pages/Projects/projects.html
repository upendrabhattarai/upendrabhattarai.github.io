Research

Where computational meets molecular

I work at the intersection of multi-omics integration and molecular biology — applying genomics, transcriptomics, and epigenomics to questions in neurodevelopmental disorders, oncology, developmental biology, and host–parasite dynamics. My work combines short and long read sequencing, computational rigor, and hands-on wet-lab experience to translate complex high-dimensional data into biological insight.

Methods

Computational & experimental toolbox

The methods I keep coming back to — building references, profiling expression, mapping regulation, and asking how parasites rewire their hosts.

Genomics

De novo genome assembly, annotation, and comparative genomics — leveraging short and long read sequencing (PacBio / Nanopore) to build high-quality references across diverse species.

Transcriptomics

Bulk and single-cell RNA-seq for differential expression, alternative splicing, and cell-state inference — across temporal and spatial scales.

Epigenomics

DNA methylation (WGBS) and chromatin profiling (ChIP-seq, CUT&RUN, accessibility) to decode regulatory programs underlying biological complexity.

Host–parasite

Integrative analyses to understand the molecular mechanisms of host behavioural manipulation and the evolutionary dynamics of parasite–host interactions.

Application areas

Where the methods get applied

The biological questions I bring computational and experimental approaches to — both in current translational neuroscience work and across previous and ongoing collaborations.

Translational neuroscience

Multi-omics integration for neurodegenerative disease research at the Center for Translational Neuroscience — supporting clinical and laboratory teams in turning sequencing data into biologically meaningful insight.

Oncology

Functional profiling of cell-cycle regulators (e.g., p53 and RB) and mechanism-driven molecular stratification — collaborating on cancer genomics studies that connect phenotype to therapeutic strategy.

Developmental biology

Comparative genomics and transcriptomics of emerging model organisms — including chromosome-level assemblies for hemimetabolous insects to study temporal and spatial gene regulation.
