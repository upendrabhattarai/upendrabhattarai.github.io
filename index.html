Senior Data Scientist · Computational research scientist

Center for Translational Neuroscience · Brown University

I work at the nexus of high-dimensional data integration and molecular biology, specializing in the synthesis of multi-omic layers to decode complex cellular programs. My approach leverages a deep-rooted foundation in wet-lab experimentation to inform computational rigor, allowing me to translate massive sequencing datasets into actionable biological narratives. From the nuances of neurodevelopment and oncology to the intricacies of host–parasite dynamics, I focus on building the frameworks that turn "big data" into an understanding of life's functional architecture.

Selected projects

MSc · 2010 — 2012

Fatal attraction: fungal biocontrol of spider mites

Norwegian University of Life Sciences · Norway

I worked with the two-spotted spider mite (Tetranychus urticae) — a globally damaging crop pest of strawberries, vegetables, and greenhouse crops — and its specialist fungal pathogen Neozygites floridana, a promising biological control agent. We found that male mites are "fatally attracted" to female cadavers killed by the fungus, approaching and even guarding them more readily than healthy living females, only retreating once infective spores became visible. This sensory trap accelerates pathogen transmission across mite populations — insight that helps growers in Norway and worldwide deploy N. floridana as a more efficient, sustainable alternative to chemical acaricides for biological control of spider mites (Trandem, Bhattarai et al., Journal of Invertebrate Pathology, 2015).

PhD · 2014 — 2018

Cracking the 130-year mystery of baculovirus tree-top disease

Northwest A&F University · China

I worked with Asian gypsy moth larvae (Lymantria dispar asiatica) and their pathogenic baculovirus LdMNPV — the cause of "tree-top disease," a long-standing behavioural mystery in which infected caterpillars climb to elevated positions before death. We delivered the first host-side molecular explanation: circadian entrainment and phototransduction are the key signalling pathways during infection, the core clock genes (per, tim) lose their normal day/night rhythm, and the larvae become positively phototactic — actively climbing toward light. The work also produced the first transcriptomic resource for the host response in this system. These findings now inform field deployment of baculoviruses as biological pesticides against gypsy moth and Spodoptera in forestry and agriculture, where photoperiod and timing materially shape efficacy (Bhattarai et al., Scientific Reports, 2018).

PhD · 2019 — 2023

A reference genome and an eDNA window into host–parasite biology

University of Otago · New Zealand

I worked with the European earwig (Forficula auricularia) and its mermithid nematode parasites — a system famous for the bizarre suicidal water-seeking behaviour the parasite drives in its host. We delivered the first chromosome-level reference genome for the species (a 1.06 Gb hybrid Nanopore + 10x linked-read assembly — only the second whole genome ever published across the entire Dermaptera order), resolved the New Zealand population as subspecies B, and uncovered an unusually expanded repeatome (~65% of the genome — nearly twice that of other hemimetabolous insects of similar size). We also developed a non-invasive method that detects endoparasitic infection from host droppings via environmental DNA, removing the need to dissect or sacrifice individuals. The genome is now a public resource for research on maternal care, sociality, sexual selection, and host–parasite interactions across dermapterans, and the eDNA approach is being adopted for ecological parasite surveys and agricultural pest monitoring (Bhattarai et al. 2022, G3; Doherty & Bhattarai 2023, MER).

Postdoc · 2022 — 2024

Hemimetabolous insect genomics

Extavour Lab · Harvard University

Developed five hemimetabolous insect species as new genetic model organisms. Generated chromosome-level genome assemblies and full annotations, then ran comparative genomics and transcriptomics across temporal and spatial scales to map evolutionary patterns of gene regulation (Kataoka et al. 2026, G3 — Gryllus bimaculatus).

Cancer genomics · 2024 — 2025

Endometrial carcinoma molecular stratification

Harvard Chan Bioinformatics Core · Collaboration

Functional profiling of p53 and RB cell-cycle regulators across endometrial carcinoma. Contributed mechanism-driven molecular stratification linking regulatory proficiency to phenotype, supporting more rational therapy assignment (Yang et al., Cancer Research Communications, 2025).
